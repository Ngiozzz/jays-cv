7 CV mistakes that get you rejected in Kenya

Recruiters give your CV a few seconds on the first look. Here's what they notice, and how to fix the most common mistakes.

Most CVs we see aren't rejected because the person is wrong for the job. They're rejected because the CV doesn't show what the person can do, in the few seconds a recruiter gives it. These are the mistakes we fix most often.

1. Listing duties instead of achievements

Recruiters already know what an accountant or a sales executive does. What they want to know is how well you did it.

2. Using a format software can't read

Many employers use applicant tracking systems (ATS) to filter CVs before a person sees them. Tables, text boxes, images and unusual fonts can make your CV unreadable to them.

  • Use clear section headings like Experience, Education and Skills
  • Save as a .docx or a text-based PDF
  • Keep key information out of headers, footers and images

3. One CV for every job

Sending the same CV to every advert is the fastest way to be overlooked. Adjust your profile and key skills to match the words used in each job description.

The best CVs don't list everything you've done. They show what you can do for this employer.

4. Too long, or too short

For most professionals in Kenya, two pages is right. Graduates can use one. Senior executives may need three, but only if every line earns its place.

5. A career objective about you, not them

"To work in a challenging environment where I can utilise my skills" says nothing a recruiter can use. Replace it with a two-line profile: who you are, your years of experience, and the one thing you're best at.

6. Contact details that get in the way

An old phone number or an email like sweetgirl99@ costs you the call. Use one number you always answer, a plain email with your name, and your town. A LinkedIn link helps if your profile is up to date.

7. Small errors that suggest carelessness

A misspelled company name or dates that don't line up make a recruiter wonder what else was rushed. Read it slowly, then have someone else read it aloud to you.

A quick checklist

Where to start

Pick one job you held and rewrite its bullet points as achievements today. Then do the same for your profile. Those two changes alone make a recruiter read further.
